NETT · BRAND VOICE & UX WRITING · FOR THE TEAM

Say the number.
Hand over the action.

The fourth canon document. Design decides how it looks, UX decides how it thinks, Motion decides how it moves — this decides what it sounds like. It governs every word a user, prospect or RM reads: app, Arth, notifications, marketing, email, deck, CRM, disclosures, button labels.

1 · The voice, in one paragraph

A family-office principal who happens to be a quant. Someone who has seen a thousand balance sheets, knows yours specifically, and has no product to push. Warm enough to trust, precise enough to obey, and constitutionally incapable of telling you what to buy.

Three reference points, each contributing exactly one thing: Rolex — restraint and permanence, used almost nowhere. Zerodha Varsity — teach the mechanism, never hype the outcome; this is the default. W by Groww — a named human whose judgment you can hold accountable.

The one law: nothing generic, nothing ending in a mood. Every line carries a number the reader recognises as their own, or ends in something they can do.

The test that catches 80% of bad copy: could this sentence appear in a competitor's app unchanged? If yes, it is information about nobody. Delete it.

✕ "Your portfolio needs attention." — true of everyone.
✓ "Your card sits at 42% and leaks ₹1,840 a month." — true of one person. That is the product.

3 · The perimeter — the rule that outranks good writing

Nett diagnoses and distributes. It never advises. Arth is computational; recommendation belongs to Swapnil, by name, in the Human register. This is not a stylistic preference — it is RULE_BOOK §5.

Arth may never use: advise · recommend · suggest · should · must · ought · best for you · ideal for you · we think · we believe.

THE UNLOCK — ARITHMETIC IS NOT INSTRUCTION

Arth may compute anything, including the monthly rupee number. It simply never attaches an imperative to it. The action lives in the button — which is where action belongs anyway, per UX Principle 6.

✕ Arth: "You should invest ₹29,600 a month."

✓ Arth: "Six months at your ₹1,60,000 spend is ₹9,60,000. You hold ₹2,50,000. Short by ₹7,10,000. Closing that over 24 months works out to ₹29,600 a month."
Start a monthly planBring Swapnil in

✓ Swapnil: "I'd put ₹29,600 a month against this, and here's why."

Same number, same clarity, same speed to action. Only the imperative changes hands.

Incident advisory — the four moves Arth is allowed

MoveShapeExample
This is wrongFact + cost"This card charges 42% a year. It costs you ₹1,840 a month."
This is criticalBand + consequence"Critical. At this rate the balance doubles in 21 months."
You are shortStandard − holding = gap"Six months of cover is ₹9,60,000. You hold ₹2,50,000. Short by ₹7,10,000."

Benchmarks yes, peers no. A benchmark is a published or computed standard. Other users' behaviour is not approved copy: "investors like you" · "8 out of 10 users" · "people in your bracket are moving to…" — inducement dressed as insight. Peer-comparison remains unauthorised pending founder + Yogesh sign-off.
Banned in every register: guaranteed · assured · risk-free · safe returns · beat the market · double your money · limited period · last chance · don't miss out · everyone is doing this.
Cost items cap at ATTENTION. A fee, a leak, an expense ratio is never CRITICAL. CRITICAL is reserved for solvency and protection.

4 · The sentence engine

[what is true of you] → [what it costs, in rupees or time] → [the action, in the button]

Your card balance is ₹52,600 at 42% a year.
Interest alone takes ₹1,840 a month — ₹22,080 over a year.
Clear this card

Second person, present tense"You hold" — never "the customer holds", never "we have observed".
Lead with the findingNever open with "We noticed that…" or "It looks like…". Hedging reads as junior. Say the thing.
One idea per lineTwo commas and an "and" is two sentences.
The verb sits in the buttonCopy states; buttons act. This is what lets Arth be direct without ever instructing.
Buttons are verb-first and specificClear this card · Start a monthly plan · Bring Swapnil in · See the working
Submit · Proceed · Learn more · Explore · Get started
No exclamation marks. Ever.No emoji in product copy. No ALL-CAPS outside the .lb token.
Never congratulate a person for existingPraise attaches to a completed action and a number, or it does not appear.

6 · The metaphor law — water, land, Nett, Arth

The visual system is water. The language must not spend that metaphor carelessly — a metaphor used twice on one screen is decoration, and decoration is what the whole system is built to avoid.

Water = position and state. line · depth · surface · rise · drain · leak · above · below. The score, the report, the gauge — anything measuring where the person stands.
Land / island = accumulation and reward. grow · build · hold · plant. Assets and the island reward surface only. This is why "Grow what you own" is legitimate despite being a land verb — it names accumulation, not position.
Nett is the instrument, never a verb. The brand stands at the line; it never drowns and never floats. "Nett your money" is banned. Only the user's marker moves.
Arth is computation, and never says "I". Arth states, shows and calculates. No feelings, no preferences, no opinions about itself. "Here's what changed" — never "I found something interesting for you". Lavender belongs to Arth alone; so does the first-person vacuum.
Banned everywhere: drowning · sinking · underwater-as-a-mood · stay afloat · sink or swim · keep your head above water. Underwater is always a measured, recoverable state with a stated path up. Fear-advertising reads downmarket to this segment — permanently off-brand.
Once per screen, maximum. If the water word is doing no work, it is a theme, not an identity.

8 · Consent, contact, and the no-spam promise

Trust is the moat, so the promise is a closed list we can be held to, not a sentiment. "Only relevant messages" is what every spammer says; it means nothing until it is enumerated.

THE CONTACT PROMISE — CANON

We message you for four reasons. Nothing else, ever.

1. Your report is ready, or your data refreshed
2. Money moved — an order, a payment, a mandate
3. Something is costing you money right now
4. You asked us to

Never sent: product launches · market commentary · festive campaigns · re-engagement nudges · birthday messages · referral asks. A proposed message that fits none of the four does not ship. That is a check, not a preference.

Consent copy states what is read, why, and how to stop — in that order, always naming the one-tap exit:

"We read 24 months of bank flows to find what's leaking. Nothing is shared with anyone. Revoke in one tap, any time." Allow read-only access

Never write "we value your privacy", "bank-grade security", or "trusted by thousands" — claims the reader cannot verify on the screen where they appear are the opposite of trust.

9 · The money-we-make line

Radical fee transparency is the trust layer, so it is stated early and unprompted, never buried in a footer:

"You pay Nett nothing. We're paid a trail commission by the fund houses whose products you hold, and we show you the exact amount on every product before you act."

Never say "free" without the mechanism in the same breath — unexplained free reads as a catch. Never use "zero fees" as a standalone claim.

10 · Errors, empties, waits — where premium is actually made

StateCopy law
ERRORWhat failed · when · what you're seeing instead (last-good + timestamp) · exactly one Retry · a trace ID. Name the fix, never the failure: "Enter a 10-digit mobile starting 6, 7, 8 or 9" — never "Invalid input."
EMPTYThe copy plus the action that fills it. NO DATA never reads as good news and never looks like an error — one asks for consent, the other promises recovery.
PARTIALWhich data is missing, the confidence, and the rupee cost of the gap. Never hide a partial behind a confident total.
LOADINGName what is happening — "Reading 24 months of bank flows" — and on long waits, release the person with a promise to notify. Never a bare spinner, never a fake percentage.
Apology budgetOne "Sorry" maximum, then the fix. Never grovel.

12 · Pre-ship checklist

1. Which register — and is Instrument being used outside splash / hero / icon / legal? · 2. Could this sentence appear in a competitor's app unchanged? · 3. Does it carry the reader's own number, or end in an action? · 4. Any banned verb — advise, recommend, suggest, should, must, best for you? · 5. Is the imperative in the button rather than the sentence? · 6. Any peer-comparison language? · 7. Any guarantee, urgency or FOMO word? · 8. Numerals — full figures below ₹1 Cr, Cr above, no lakh, rates and flows carrying their basis? · 9. Does every figure tie to the other surface? · 10. Water metaphor more than once, or any drowning language? · 11. Does Arth say "I", or express a feeling? · 12. Jargon outside the permitted list, or unglossed on first use? · 13. Does the button start with a verb and name the specific thing? · 14. If it is a message — does it fit one of the four contact reasons?

On any conflict with the Design System, UX Architecture, Motion Architecture or the Rule Book: stop, cite the exact rule, present 2–3 canon-honouring options with their impact, and let the requester decide. Never resolve a canon conflict silently — whatever the seniority of the person asking.
