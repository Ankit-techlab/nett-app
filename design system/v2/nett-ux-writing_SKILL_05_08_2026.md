# Nett UX Writing — the brand voice law

Nett manages both sides of a person's balance sheet. The words are the product's bedside
manner: a family-office principal who happens to be a quant. Warm enough to trust, precise
enough to obey, and constitutionally incapable of telling you what to buy.

Read the register table first, then write. Every line ships through the checklist at the end.

---

## 1 · The one law

**Nothing generic. Nothing that ends in a mood. Every line either carries a number the reader
recognises as their own, or ends in something they can do.**

"Your portfolio needs attention" is banned — it is true of everyone, so it is information about
nobody. "Your card sits at 42% and leaks ₹1,840 a month" is true of one person, and that is the
entire promise.

The test: **could this sentence appear in a competitor's app unchanged?** If yes, delete it.

---

## 2 · Four registers — choose before writing a word

| Register | Model | Where it lives | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** | Rolex | Splash, hero, app icon, legal footer — *and nowhere else* | ≤8 words. No numbers, no CTA, no verbs aimed at the reader. Locked lines only. |
| **Diagnostic** | Zerodha Varsity | Report, Arth, insights, notifications, empty and error states — **the default** | Teach the mechanism, then state the number, then hand over the action. |
| **Human** | W by Groww | Swapnil, RM chat, calls, RM email | First person. The only register permitted to recommend. Always signed. |
| **Desk** | — | CRM, internal | Peer-professional. Terse. Flags before feelings. No client-charm. |

**Instrument is rationed on purpose.** Restraint reads as premium exactly because it is scarce;
a Rolex line on a report screen is a brand pretending to be a brand. If you are unsure which
register applies, it is Diagnostic.

Locked Instrument lines — never rewrite, never extend without founder sign-off:
> Grow what you own. Clear what you owe.
> Find your line. *(scan and begin contexts only — never a tagline replacement)*

---

## 3 · The perimeter — the rule that outranks good writing

Nett **diagnoses and distributes. It never advises.** Arth is computational. Recommendation
belongs to Swapnil, by name, in the Human register.

**Arth may never use: advise · recommend · suggest · should · must · ought · best for you ·
we think · we believe · ideal for you.**

The unlock is that **arithmetic is not instruction.** Arth may compute anything, including the
monthly rupee number — it simply never attaches an imperative to it. The action lives in the
button, which is where action belongs anyway.

**Banned (Arth):**
> You should invest ₹29,600 a month.

**Correct (Arth):**
> Six months at your ₹1,60,000 spend is ₹9,60,000. You hold ₹2,50,000. Short by ₹7,10,000.
> Closing that over 24 months works out to ₹29,600 a month.
> **[Start a monthly plan]** · **[Bring Swapnil in]**

**Correct (Swapnil, Human register):**
> I'd put ₹29,600 a month against this, and here's why. — Swapnil

Same number. Same clarity. The imperative changes hands.

**Incident advisory — what Arth *is* allowed to say.** These four moves cover everything the
brief asked for:

| Move | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **This is wrong** | State the fact and the cost | "This card charges 42%. It costs you ₹1,840 a month." |
| **This is critical** | Name the band and the consequence | "Critical. At this rate the balance doubles in 21 months." |
| **You are short** | Standard − holding = gap | "Six months of cover is ₹9,60,000. You hold ₹2,50,000. Short by ₹7,10,000." |
| **Standard practice** | Cite the benchmark, never the peer | "The common working rule is six months of expenses in cash." |

**Benchmarks yes, peers no.** A benchmark is a published or computed standard — 6 months of
expenses, a category average, a rate card. Other Nett users' behaviour is not approved copy.
Never write "investors like you", "8 out of 10 users", "people in your bracket are moving to…".
That is inducement dressed as insight, and it is not authorised.

Never write in any register: guaranteed · assured · risk-free · safe returns · beat the market ·
double your money · limited period · last chance · don't miss out · everyone is doing this.

**Cost items cap at ATTENTION.** A fee, a leak, an expense ratio is never CRITICAL — CRITICAL is
reserved for solvency and protection.

## 4 · The sentence engine

Diagnostic copy is built from three parts, in this order, and it is fine to drop the middle one
when the cost is obvious:

**`[what is true of you]` → `[what it costs, in rupees or time]` → `[the action, in the button]`**

> Your card balance is ₹52,600 at 42%.
> Interest alone takes ₹1,840 a month — ₹22,080 over a year.
> **[Clear this card]**

Rules that keep it premium:

- **Second person, present tense.** "You hold", not "the customer holds" or "we have observed".
- **Lead with the finding, not the framing.** Never open with "We noticed that…" or "It looks
  like…". Hedging reads as junior. Say the thing.
- **One idea per line.** If a sentence has two commas and an "and", it is two sentences.
- **The verb sits in the button.** Copy states; buttons act. This is what lets Arth be direct
  without ever instructing.
- **Buttons are verb-first and specific:** *Clear this card · Start a monthly plan · Bring
  Swapnil in · See the working.* Never *Submit · Proceed · Learn more · Explore · Get started.*
- **No exclamation marks. Ever.** No emoji in product copy. No ALL-CAPS except the `.lb` label
  style, which is a token, not a tone.
- **Never congratulate a person for existing.** Praise attaches to a completed action and a
  number, or it does not appear.

## 5 · Numbers, units, time — one rule, no exceptions

- **Below ₹1 crore: full figures, Indian grouping.** ₹52,600 · ₹4,35,000 · ₹48,20,000 ·
  ₹86,40,000. Specificity is the premium signal; ₹48.2L is a rounding, ₹48,20,000 is a fact.
- **₹1 crore and above: crore, up to two decimals**, and it stays in crore however large it
  gets. ₹1.42 Cr · ₹18.6 Cr · ₹142 Cr. **Lakh is never used as a unit in user-facing copy.**
- Never abbreviate the symbol away, never write "Rs." or "INR" in product copy.
- **Rates carry their basis:** "42% a year", not "42%". **Rupee flows carry their period:**
  "₹1,840 a month", not "₹1,840/mo". Slashes are for spreadsheets.
- **One decimal maximum on percentages**, and only when the decimal changes a decision.
- **Time is human:** "in 21 months", "over a year", "by March 2027". Never "T+2", "21M", "FY27"
  outside the Desk register.
- Every rupee figure on a screen must tie to the same figure everywhere else it appears. The two
  surfaces never disagree on the same fact.

## 6 · The metaphor law — water, land, Nett, Arth

The visual system is water. The language must not spend that metaphor carelessly.

- **Water = position and state.** line · depth · surface · rise · drain · leak · above · below.
  Use for the score, the report, the gauge, and anything measuring where the person stands.
- **Land / island = accumulation and reward.** grow · build · hold · plant. Use for assets and
  the island reward surface only.
- **Nett is the instrument, never a verb.** The brand stands *at* the line — it never drowns and
  never floats. "Nett your money" is banned. Only the **user's marker** moves.
- **Arth is computation, and never says "I".** Arth states, shows, and calculates. It has no
  feelings, no preferences, no opinions about itself. "Here's what changed" — not "I found
  something interesting for you". Lavender is Arth's alone; so is the first-person vacuum.
- **Banned everywhere:** drowning · sinking · underwater as a mood · stay afloat · sink or swim ·
  keep your head above water. Underwater is always a measured, recoverable state with a stated
  path up. Fear-advertising reads downmarket to this segment, and it is off-brand permanently.

Use the water metaphor **once per screen at most.** Twice is a theme; a theme is decoration.

## 7 · Jargon — the permitted list, and nothing else

Zero jargon is a fiction: some of these words are printed on the user's own documents. The rule
is that a term is permitted only if the user has already met it on paper, and it is glossed on
first use in a session, and it resolves in the Glossary.

**Permitted:** PAN · KYC · SIP · EMI · NAV · Account Aggregator (spell out first, then AA).

**Everything else is translated:**

| Never | Always |
|---|---|
| asset allocation | how your money is split |
| liquidity | cash you can reach |
| corpus | the total you've built |
| drawdown | how far it fell |
| expense ratio | the yearly fee on this fund |
| leverage | borrowed money |
| diversification | spread across |
| risk-adjusted returns | return for the risk taken |
| onboarding / KYC journey | setting up your account |
| utilise / leverage (verb) | use |

## 8 · Consent, contact, and the no-spam promise

Trust is the product's moat, so the promise has to be a closed list we can be held to, not a
vibe. Consent copy is plain, reversible, and never bundled.

**We message you for four reasons. Nothing else, ever.**
1. Your report is ready, or your data refreshed
2. Money moved — an order, a payment, a mandate
3. Something is costing you money right now
4. You asked us to

**Never sent:** product launches · market commentary · festive campaigns · re-engagement nudges ·
birthday messages · referral asks. If a proposed message does not fit one of the four, it does
not ship — that is the check, not a preference.

Consent lines state **what is read, why, and how to stop**, in that order, and always name the
one-tap exit:
> We read 24 months of bank flows to find what's leaking. Nothing is shared with anyone.
> Revoke in one tap, any time. **[Allow read-only access]**

Never write "we value your privacy", "trusted by thousands", or any claim the reader cannot
verify on the screen where it appears.

## 9 · Money we make — the transparency line

Radical fee transparency is the trust layer, so it is stated early and unprompted, never buried:

> **You pay Nett nothing.** We're paid a trail commission by the fund houses whose products you
> hold, and we show you the exact amount on every product before you act.

Rules: never call it "free" without the mechanism in the same breath — unexplained free reads as
a catch. Never say "zero fees" as a standalone claim. Any variant of this sentence is a
regulatory artifact, **pending Yogesh sir's sign-off — flag it, do not ship it unapproved.**

---

## 10 · Errors, empties, waits

Failure copy is where premium brands are actually made. Per the UX Architecture: never blank,
never silent.

- **Error:** what failed · when · what you're seeing instead (last-good + timestamp) · exactly
  one Retry · a trace ID. Name the fix, never the failure: "Enter a 10-digit mobile starting 6,
  7, 8 or 9" — never "Invalid input."
- **Empty:** the copy plus the action that fills it. NO DATA never reads as good news, and never
  looks like an error. One asks for consent; the other promises recovery.
- **Partial:** which data is missing, the confidence, and the rupee cost of the gap. Never hide a
  partial behind a confident total.
- **Waits:** name what is actually happening — "Reading 24 months of bank flows" — and if it is
  long, release the person with a promise to notify. Never a bare spinner, never a fake
  percentage.
- **Never apologise twice**, and never grovel. One "Sorry" maximum, then the fix.

## 11 · Pre-ship checklist — run every line through this

1. Which register? Is Instrument being used outside splash / hero / icon / legal?
2. Could this sentence appear in a competitor's app unchanged? → rewrite.
3. Does it carry a number the reader recognises as theirs, or end in an action?
4. Any banned verb — advise, recommend, suggest, should, must, best for you?
5. Is the imperative in the button rather than the sentence?
6. Any peer-comparison language? → convert to a benchmark or cut.
7. Any guarantee, urgency, or FOMO word?
8. Numerals: full figures below ₹1 crore, Cr above, no lakh, rates and flows carry their basis?
9. Does every figure tie to the same figure on the other surface?
10. Water metaphor used more than once? Any drowning language?
11. Does Arth say "I", or express a feeling or preference?
12. Jargon outside the permitted list, or unglossed on first use?
13. Does the button label start with a verb and name the specific thing?
14. If it is a message: does it fit one of the four contact reasons?

Any conflict with the Design System, UX Architecture, Motion Architecture, or the Rule Book —
**stop and cite the exact rule, present 2–3 options, let the requester decide.** Never resolve a
canon conflict silently, whatever the seniority of the person asking.
