Direction C · The waterline — your concept, built out

Nett is a depth gauge.

The line is water level. Everything you own holds you above it; everything you owe pulls you under. Nett tells you one thing before anything else: how far above — or below — the line you are. The identity makes that measurement visible: the wordmark itself sits in the water.

01 · Primary wordmark — half in the water

The lower third is submerged — shifted 3 units, blue, faded: refraction. Bone above the line, water-blue below. The name demonstrates the product.

Why this works harder than rounds 1–2: the mark now contains depth, not just a line. Above/below isn't a diagram next to the name — it happens to the name. And it's honest to the product: most of your users start partly underwater. The brand doesn't pretend otherwise; it measures.

02 · The gauge — the brand's signature graphic

One graphic, every surface

· Marketing: "Where's your line?" — gauge with anonymous markers scattered above and below; the scan finds yours.

· Report page: your marker, your depth, your distance to the surface — "₹6.2L of card debt is what's holding you under."

· Ritual reward: the marker rising is the animation. Clearing a loan = watching yourself surface. Depth makes progress felt, not just counted.

· Island link: the existing island's waterline is this same line — the 3D island becomes the "see as island" render of the gauge. One metaphor, two depths of detail.

State discipline — the rule that keeps this safe

The brand is always drawn at the line — it's the instrument, it never drowns and never floats. Only the user's marker moves above or below. Marketing never depicts a submerged wordmark as a mood; underwater is always a measured, recoverable state with a path up. This is what keeps depth motivating instead of shaming — and keeps the brand out of "you're drowning in debt" fear-advertising territory, which your affluent segment reads as downmarket.
