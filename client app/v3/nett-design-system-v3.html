1 · Brand core

One idea: the line.

Nett measures how far above or below the waterline a person is. Assets hold you up; liabilities pull you under. Every design decision serves that single reading. Three principles govern everything:

The brand is the instrument

The Nett mark always stands at the line. It never drowns, never floats. Only the user's marker moves. Underwater is a measured, recoverable state — always shown with distance-to-surface and one action that closes it. Never fear imagery.

Number + action

Every line of product copy either carries a number or ends in an action. No mood copy, no vague reassurance. This is law, inherited from the product, applied to the brand.

Diagnose, never advise

Nett observes, calculates, compares, quantifies, educates, discloses, routes. The words advise / recommend / suggest never appear in Nett or Arth copy. Advice language belongs to the RM on record, by name.

2 · Logo — guideline & logic

2.0 · Logic — why the mark is half in the water

The product is a measurement

Nett tells you one thing before anything else: how far above or below the line you are. Assets hold you up; liabilities pull you under. The identity doesn’t illustrate that idea next to the name — it happens to the name. The wordmark is the instrument.

Honest to the user

Most users start partly underwater — a home loan, a card revolve. The brand doesn’t pretend otherwise; it measures. The lower third of the mark is submerged because that is the truthful starting state of the balance sheet it serves.

The brand never drowns

The mark is always drawn at the line — it never sinks, never floats. Only the user’s marker moves. Underwater is always a measured, recoverable state with a path up. This one rule keeps depth motivating instead of shaming, and keeps Nett out of fear-advertising territory.

Derivation chain, in one line: tagline (own / owe) → the line → water level → wordmark standing in it → gauge → island. Every brand surface is the same metaphor at a different zoom.

2.3 · Misuse — hard no's

Never

Sink the mark below the line or float it above — the brand lives at the surface, always.

Never

Recolor the submerged slice sage or blush. Water is water-blue; money colors are for money.

Never

All-caps NETT, letterspaced NETT, lowercase nett, "Nett!" — the mark is title case, tracking −0.036em, and it is never typed as decoration in running copy.

Never

Waves, ripples, splash effects, fish, boats. The water is still. It's a measurement, not a seascape.

Never

Rotate, outline, add glow/shadow to the mark, or place it on photography without an ink scrim ≥ 0.75.

Never

Pair the mark with the old island render, Nuvyra gold letterspaced style, or any Felix-era asset.

2.5 · The mark in motion

· Splash: water rises to the 50% line in 1.4s (--ease), the wordmark is already at the surface — water comes to the brand, the brand never moves.
· The mark itself is never animated beyond a single fade-in. No bobbing, no ripple across the letters — motion belongs to water and markers, not the instrument.
· Shipped reference: .spwater + .wmark in the app prototype.

4.2 · What changes beyond tokens — and what never does

Ink — the original

The shipped theme. Ambient radial light at ≤8%, shadows only on frame and sheets, waterline mark in full color. This is the demo theme, the deck theme, and the default forever.

White — daylight

Logo switches to the §2.1 light-background variant (Ink wordmark, #4A6EA8 slice at .5, amber line unchanged). Cards gain a soft shadow (they float on paper; hairlines alone vanish in daylight). Ambient gradients drop to zero. Depth wash inverts to tint. Splash stays Ink — the brand moment is always dark water.

Black — OLED

Ink with exactly three overrides: bg, surface, hairlines (+.02 visibility). Ambient radial gradients are removed entirely — they smear on OLED. Nothing else may fork. A Black theme that diverges further than these three lines is a defect.

Theme-invariant, always: money semantics (sage=grow, blush=owe, amber=the line), lavender exclusivity, the waterline metaphor, NO DATA as dashed+dim, type scale, spacing, tap targets, copy, and all motion tokens. A theme changes the light in the room — never the instrument.

7.3 · The Arth glyph — the one owned icon

Horizon glyph — circle crossed by the line.
Upper half filled (grow), lower clearing (clear).

· Construction: circle stroke 2.5 at 40vb, full-width line through center, upper semicircle filled at .8 opacity.
· Color: var(--lav), exclusively — the glyph is never bone, water, sage, or blush.
· Sizes: 13px inline attribution · 18–19px headers · min 16px in tap targets.
· Motion: none, except a single .4s fade-in. The glyph never spins, pulses, or “thinks”.
· Placement: wherever Arth computed the content — chat header, insight attributions, pre-filled fields, report annotations. Its presence is the disclosure.
· Hard rule: the glyph on a surface Arth didn’t produce, or lavender without the glyph’s context, are both review blockers (§3.2).

7.4 · Icon misuse — hard no’s

Never

Icon-only actions on money surfaces. Every executing control carries words; an icon may accompany, never replace.

Never

Emoji as iconography, duotone/filled sets beyond the two exceptions, or a second icon library “just for one screen”.

Never

Coloring an icon for emphasis when the state hasn’t changed — semantic color is earned by state, not attention-seeking.

Never

Redrawing the Arth glyph, adding a face, eyes, or sparkles to it, or using any “AI sparkle” iconography anywhere in the product.

Horizon glyph — circle crossed by the line; upper half filled (grow), lower clearing (clear). Stroke 2.5 at 40vb; min size 16px; always lavender; never animated except a single .4s fade-in.

12.2 · Copy laws

1 · Every line carries a number or ends in an action.
2 · Diagnose, never advise — observations, calculations, benchmarks, risk quantification, education, disclosure, routing. Nothing else.
3 · Buttons name outcomes with amounts: "Confirm — ₹25,000", never "Submit".
4 · An action keeps its name through the flow: "Clear card" button → "Card cleared" toast.
5 · Fees in full, same format, every time: rate% · ₹/yr.
6 · Depth copy is measured, never moody: "₹6.2L below the line — clearing the card brings you ₹6.2L closer to the surface." Not "drowning in debt".
7 · NO DATA states name the missing rail and the fix: "DEMAT not linked · Link now". Never blank.
8 · Sentence case everywhere except .lb labels. No exclamation marks. No emoji in product.

13.2 · Engineering rules

1 · Tokens only — no raw hex in component code. A hex outside this sheet is a review blocker.
2 · Waterline mark ships as SVG components (full + dry variants) with the clip-path construction in §2.1 — never rebuilt in CSS text tricks.
3 · font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums on every money element.
4 · Click delegation selector must keep covering all tap classes (.tap,.lnk,.seg span,[data-*]…) — new interactive components register a data attribute, they don't invent handlers.
5 · One .cta per screen, enforced in review.
6 · Lavender outside Arth surfaces is a review blocker. 6b · Themes are token remaps only (§4) — a component that branches on theme beyond tokens is a defect; White/Black do not ship until signed off.
7 · prefers-reduced-motion respected on every animation token.
8 · NO DATA renders dashed + dim; a filled or colored NO DATA chip is a defect.
9 · Ship sequence unchanged: patch with assertions → section-presence check (all 50 screen IDs) → handler reachability → runtime smoke test → outputs.
